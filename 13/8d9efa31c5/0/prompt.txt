## 3. Rust-specific failures

- **`impl` blocks are not containers, and this is the headline bug.** `def deletion` proves the symbol is indexed. `def Edit` proves it is not associated with its type. So "what can I do with an `Edit`?" — the single most common Rust question — is unanswerable, while the data to answer it is already in the index. In Rust, a type's public API *is* its `impl` blocks; a graph that indexes `struct` and `fn` separately and never joins them has indexed Rust as if it were C.
- **Wrong-type members ranked as answers.** `def Edit` returned `fix.rs:48 field edits` and `fix.rs:146 method edits` — members of `Fix`, not `Edit`. That is substring matching wearing a structural costume, and in Rust (where `Fix` and `Edit` are adjacent, related types) it is exactly the confusion that produces a wrong patch.

## 8. Cost intuition — and my own greed

A turn replays ~40 k tokens of context; a returned byte is replayed on every subsequent turn. So a byte returned at turn 2 of a 17-turn session is paid ~15 times, and a turn eliminated is worth roughly 40 k / (bytes it would have cost) — call it 10 KB of returned payload per turn saved before it's a wash.

**Worth their bytes:**
- Call-site line numbers + the `INVARIANT:` guard chain: ~250 bytes, replaced ~2.6 KB of `sed` output *and* 2 turns. ~30× return. Best item on the list.
- `impl` method signature list for signature-types only: ~300 bytes, replaced 3 turns and 1.5 KB. Very cheap because I need *names and arg types*, never bodies.
- Fixture/snapshot paths + test command: ~150 bytes, saved 1.5 turns. Nearly free.
- The 4006-byte `search`: worth every byte, and the one place I'd spend *more* — a 6 KB first response that ends exploration is strictly cheaper than a 2 KB one that costs a second turn.

**Not worth their bytes — including things I want and shouldn't get:**
- Full caller bodies. `expression.rs`'s dispatch fn is ~1400 lines / ~55 KB. A 10-line window is 100× cheaper and *more* useful. Never inline a caller body; always window it.
- My instinct to ask for "the impl surface of every type in scope" — in Rust that's the transitive closure of `ruff_python_ast`, hundreds of KB, replayed every turn. Hard cap: types appearing in the **target symbol's own signature**, signatures only, no bodies, ≤15 methods per type, truncate with a count.
- The `Completeness` block: ~200 bytes × every call, negative information value. Delete or one-line it.
- `callers`' 26 s reindex: costs no tokens but costs wall-clock and tempted me to batch two queries into one command, which is how I ended up making a redundant call. Read the prebuilt graph like `search` does.
- I'd also *like* co-change history and sibling-rule diffs. On this task: worth zero. Don't build it for Rust before you've joined `impl` to `struct`.

Net for this task: the graph saved me roughly **2 turns** on localization (real, measurable — one call replaced a 2–3 call ripgrep funnel) and **cost me ~1.5 turns** on relation queries that were redundant, wrongly anchored, or false. Fixing item 1 and item 2 in §5 flips that from ≈break-even to a clear 4–5 turn win, and both are joins over data the index already contains.</result>
