## Why my own proposal was wrong

I read the lock as a check-then-emit guard and said: read without the lock, re-verify the policy, then answer. The comment at `retrieval_transport.go:113` says what it actually is:

> *"`withLockedRetrievalPrivacyPolicies` is the response linearization boundary. Tombstone mutations use the same per-Brain write lock."*

It is held **across the byte write itself** (`out.Write(data)` at `:104`), not just around the identity check. The guarantee is about **erasure**: once a privacy tombstone is being applied, no response may be emitted carrying that data. An optimistic re-verify leaves a window between the re-check and the write where a tombstone can land — small, nonzero, and in an erasure guarantee that is the one place a window is unacceptable. So: withdrawn. The reader's lock should stay exactly as it is.

A shared lock is wrong for a second reason too, beyond still waiting for writers: readers hold this lock to *linearize against writers*, which is precisely what a shared lock stops doing.

## What is actually happening

The reader takes the lock at **response-write time**, i.e. at the very end of the brief, and holds it for microseconds. So the 10.5 s failure is a reader that did all its work and then could not emit — maximally wasteful, and nothing to do with how the reader locks.
